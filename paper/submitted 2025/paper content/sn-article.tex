\abstract{Affecting more than 1 in 7 American adults, the prevalence of Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) cannot be understated. Modeling CKD progression is crucial in identifying patients who are at high risk for end stage renal disease (ESRD), a disease with high mortality rates. However, existing models often make unrealistic assumptions such as time invariance, complete patient feature monitoring, and a reliance on pre-defined measures such as eGFR. Such an environment in general hospital settings are rare. Currently, no CKD progression benchmarks exist to test model performance under these scenarios. To address this challenge, we construct a new large-scale CKD survival analysis benchmark dataset extracted from the large MIMIC-IV dataset with over 10,000 patients and design several benchmark scenarios to better understand how survival analysis models perform across different feature sets such as time variant, invariant, and across a wide range of heterogeneous as well as homogeneous features. In our benchmarking, we show that the incorporation of time variant features directly improves model performance by upwards of 20\%, and that while deep models outperform their traditional counterparts, the architectures need not be complex for survival analysis. We make our benchmark widely accessible through PyHealth (at \url{https://github.com/sunlabuiuc/PyHealth}).}
