\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) affects over 1 in 7 American adults and can progress to end-stage renal disease (ESRD) \citep{CDC2023CKD}. Early identification of high-risk patients is critical for nephrology referral and dialysis-access planning \citep{levin2011early_detection}. The clinical standard for this task is the Kidney Failure Risk Equation (KFRE) \citep{tangri2011predicting}, a closed-form Cox model built from as few as four routinely collected variables (age, sex, eGFR, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (uACR)), or eight in its extended form (adding calcium, phosphate, bicarbonate, albumin). KFRE has been externally validated across 31 cohorts spanning over 700{,}000 patients \citep{tangri2016multinational} and is embedded in clinical guidelines \citep{major2019kfre_external_validation}.

KFRE's minimalism is a deliberate design choice, not a data limitation: it was derived under classical regression sample-size constraints from a handful of clinically available variables. Modern EHR resources and expressive deep survival architectures raise a question KFRE's own literature does not answer: \emph{does adding more labs and more flexible models meaningfully improve on it, or does the 4--8 variable KFRE feature set already capture most of the recoverable signal?} Existing CKD deep-learning benchmarks conflate this question, varying feature count and model architecture together.

We isolate it on a shared MIMIC-IV \citep{johnson2023mimic_ehr} CKD cohort, holding the population fixed and varying only the feature set across three nested regimes: \textbf{four\_features} and \textbf{eight\_features} (exact reconstructions of KFRE's 4- and 8-variable inputs) and \textbf{twenty\_features} (a heterogeneous 20-lab, missingness-flagged stress test with no published KFRE analogue), benchmarking 10 survival models per scenario plus the closed-form KFRE equation itself on the two KFRE-comparable scenarios (11 total there).

Building KFRE-comparable cohorts required a row-merge design not needed when every input is a single lab type: KFRE's inputs are multiple, asynchronously-drawn labs that must be combined into simultaneous covariate snapshots without collapsing a patient's repeated measurements into one row (Section~\ref{sec:cohort}).

\textbf{Contributions.} (1) A cohort-construction methodology for merging multiple asynchronously-drawn labs into KFRE-comparable, patient-level landmark rows, each design choice backed by prior clinical/statistical literature. (2) A direct benchmark of 10 survival models plus published KFRE across three feature-set sizes on the same cohort, with discrimination, calibration, and decision-curve analysis, aggregated across independent repetitions. (3) A methodological finding: evaluating time-varying survival models on raw per-visit rows rather than one prediction per patient inflates discrimination for several architectures, including KFRE itself — a pitfall likely shared by other EHR time-to-event benchmarks reusing a counting-process row format. (4) A disclosed, investigated limitation: a Cox model performing below chance on this row format, reported openly rather than hidden.
