\textbf{Feature count barely moves most models.} Nine of eleven models change by $<$0.03 C-index between four\_features and eight\_features, including KFRE (0.539 on both); only Cox ($+$0.16) and Hazard Transformer ($-$0.06) move meaningfully, in opposite directions.

\textbf{KFRE is not clearly beaten by most learned models.} On equal per-patient footing, KFRE's C-index lands squarely inside the cluster formed by Logistic Hazard, RNN-Surv, DeepSurv, GBSA, Survival RF, Survival SVM, and Weibull AFT (0.49--0.58) — none of which reliably outperforms the published clinical equation. Only \textbf{Dynamic-DeepHit} clearly beats it (C-index 0.83--0.87, AUC 0.94--0.95), consistent with being the only benchmarked model that consumes each patient's full multi-visit sequence rather than a single landmark row.

\textbf{Cox underperforms and remains an open question.} Cox scores \emph{below} the 0.5 random baseline on four\_features (0.308) and near-random on eight\_features (0.470) — the only model to do so on either scenario, and not yet explained by an architectural cause we can point to (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{Feature Importance, Calibration, Decision Curves}
Figure~\ref{fig:feature_importance_four} shows each model's native importance measure (Cox/Weibull/SVM: $|$coefficient$|$; GBSA: impurity-based; SRF: permutation importance; remaining neural models: mean$|$gradient$|$ — not SHAP values, despite this analysis's inherited file naming). eGFR dominates for models that use it at all (0.25--0.46); uACR's contribution is architecture-dependent, substantial for GBSA/DeepSurv but effectively zero for every linear/kernel model (Cox, SVM, Weibull all assign it 0.0000) — the same family of models that underperforms on discrimination.

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figs/four_features_all_models_feature_importance.png}
    \caption{Per-model feature importance, four\_features.}
    \label{fig:feature_importance_four}
\end{figure}

Dynamic-DeepHit is the best-calibrated model at both 2- and 5-year horizons; Cox is calibrated backwards at 2 years (predicted risk rises while observed risk falls) — the calibration-level symptom of its below-chance discrimination. For decision-curve analysis, every model except Cox exceeds the eGFR-threshold referral rule's net benefit across the clinically relevant 0.05--0.30 threshold range — the clearest evidence here that model-guided referral beats a simple eGFR-cutoff rule already used in practice, and the one result that does not depend on resolving Cox's open question.

\subsection{Twenty Features}
Table~\ref{tab:twenty} reports discrimination for the 20-feature heterogeneous scenario (10 models, no KFRE analogue). The same pattern reappears: Dynamic-DeepHit leads by a wide margin, and Cox (and here, more severely, Hazard Transformer) score below chance while every other model stays at or above random — evidence the Cox/Hazard-Transformer issue is not specific to the four/eight-feature merge design, though a common root cause has not been isolated.
