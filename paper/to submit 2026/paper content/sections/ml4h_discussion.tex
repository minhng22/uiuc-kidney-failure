\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{More features did not clearly help.} Adding calcium, phosphate, bicarbonate, and albumin to the 4-variable KFRE set left nine of eleven models within 0.03 C-index of their four-feature result. Read with KFRE's own middling position among the ten learned models, this favors the hypothesis that most recoverable signal in a small, clinically curated feature set is already present at four variables — mirroring KFRE's original design intuition — with Dynamic-DeepHit's full-sequence architecture, not feature count, the source of its advantage.

\textbf{Evaluation unit matters as much as model choice.} The reversal between row-level and patient-level evaluation (Section~\ref{sec:eval}) is, in our view, the most transferable finding here, independent of which model wins. Any survival benchmark built on a counting-process row format should confirm, per model, whether test-time scoring happens once per patient or once per row, since the two can rank models differently, as they did here for KFRE and Cox.

\textbf{Decision-curve analysis is robust to the open Cox question.} Regardless of how Cox's below-chance discrimination is eventually explained, nearly every model exceeds a simple eGFR-threshold referral rule's net benefit — the clearest actionable finding here, and the one least contingent on that open question.

\subsection{Limitations} \label{sec:limitations}
\textbf{Cox's below-chance discrimination is unexplained.} It is consistent across all three feature-set scenarios, ruling out a scenario-specific data bug, but we have not isolated whether the cause is model non-convergence, a sign/encoding issue specific to this row format, or something else; Cox's numbers here should be read as evidence that \emph{something} in this representation is unusually hard for a linear proportional-hazards model, not as a validated statement that Cox is simply worse here. \textbf{No competing-risks modeling}: death before ESRD is censored, not modeled as a competing event, since recovering each subject's absolute calendar anchor was out of this pipeline's current scope. \textbf{Feature importance is model-native, not SHAP}: gradient saliency, permutation importance, and raw coefficients are not on a common scale across model families. Full architectural detail, cohort-merge derivation, and citations for every design choice above are in the Appendix.
