FINDING #1 (CONFIRMED, root-caused): eGFR-referral-rule net benefit is wrong
for twenty_features_heterogeneous
================================================================================
Symptom: `eGFR-threshold referral rule net benefit` in
twenty_features_heterogeneous_clinical_validity_report.txt is {30: -18.46,
45: -25.81} (730d) / {30: -18.00, 45: -25.17} (1825d) — three orders of
magnitude outside the sane range every other number in every report falls
in (four_features: -0.037/-0.140; eight_features: +0.071/+0.012).

Root cause: `egfr_threshold_net_benefit()`
(pkgs/data_analysis/clinical_validity_analysis.py:214-240) is called with
`df_test['egfr'].values` directly
(pkgs/data_analysis/clinical_validity_analysis.py:820-822), with no
filtering on `egfr_missing`. For four_features/eight_features every row is
anchored on a real creatinine draw, so `egfr` is always a genuine
measurement there and the function is correct as written. But
twenty_features_heterogeneous is one row per lab EVENT
(pkgs/data_analysis/time_series_store.py:379-421): only rows where the
drawn lab *was* creatinine/eGFR get a real `egfr` value
(`egfr_missing=0`); every other row (potassium draw, sodium draw, etc.) gets
`egfr=0` as a placeholder with `egfr_missing=1`. Verified directly against
generated_data/rep99/twenty_features_heterogeneous_test_data.csv: 8,613 of
9,115 test rows (94.5%) have `egfr_missing=1` and `egfr=0`.

Since `high_risk = egfr_values < cutoff` treats a placeholder 0 exactly like
a genuine severely-low eGFR, ~94.5% of rows are misclassified as
"eGFR<cutoff, refer" regardless of cutoff (30 or 45). That pushes `n_high/n`
to ~0.95+, so `implied_pt = n_high/n` -> ~0.95, and the net-benefit formula's
`implied_pt/(1-implied_pt)` term (line 239) explodes toward ~19-20x —
exactly matching the observed -18 to -25 magnitude.

This is isolated to the eGFR-referral-rule *comparator* line in the
twenty_features_heterogeneous report. It does not affect: any model's
C-index/Brier/AUC/calibration/decision-curve numbers (those don't call this
function); four_features/eight_features (egfr is never a placeholder
there); or any other scenario's comparator line.

Proposed fix: in `analyze_scenario()`
(pkgs/data_analysis/clinical_validity_analysis.py:757), when an
`egfr_missing` column exists, restrict the eGFR-referral-rule computation to
rows with `egfr_missing == 0` (the rows where eGFR was actually measured)
instead of the full `df_test`. Scoped to the `has_egfr` branch only — no
change to any model's own predictions/metrics, no change to
four_features/eight_features (no `egfr_missing` column there, so behavior
is unchanged for those two scenarios).

FINDING #2 (CONFIRMED PATTERN, already partially documented): the generic
"approximate" risk-score-to-probability transform saturates for 6 of 11
models, in every scenario
================================================================================
Symptom: every <scenario>_clinical_validity_report.txt repeatedly logs
"NOTE: predicted risk is the same X for every patient at this horizon" for
Cox, DeepSurv, GBSA, Survival RF, Survival SVM, and Weibull AFT (the 6
models whose report line says "Predicted-probability source: approximate
(generic risk-score extrapolation)") — X is typically 0.95-1.0 (rarely, as
in DeepSurv's four_features case, ~0.02, i.e. saturated the other
direction). Their calibration tables collapse to 1-2 rows instead of 10
deciles, and their decision-curve net benefit values are implausibly close
to the "treat-all" ceiling (e.g. 0.79-0.93 net benefit at low thresholds,
near the cohort's actual, already-noted-as-skewed high event rate) —
because "predict near-certain event for everyone" IS what treat-all does.

Root cause: `risk_scores_to_survival_probs()`
(pkgs/data_analysis/clinical_validity_analysis.py:80-88, same formula as
pkgs/experiments/utils.py's `compute_brier_score_from_risk_scores`) computes
`S(t) = exp(-(risk_score - risk_score.min() + 0.01) * t / 365)` — a single
hardcoded scale with no adaptation to a given model's actual risk-score
range. Different architectures' raw risk scores span very different
magnitudes (e.g. a Cox partial hazard vs. an SVM decision score vs. a GBSA
score), so for models whose scores span more than roughly single digits
after min-subtraction, `exp(-large * t/365)` collapses to ~0 survival (~1.0
event probability) for nearly the whole cohort except the single
minimum-risk row — exactly the reported symptom. This is not new — the
module's own docstring already calls this transform "approximate" and a
"fallback" vs. each model's "native" output — but Stage 2.2 is the first
place this repo's plan explicitly asks whether results are
publication-ready, and for these 6 models' calibration/Brier/DCA numbers
(not their C-index, which is rank-based and unaffected by this rescaling),
the honest answer is no.

This is a bigger design decision than finding #1, not a one-line fix —
flagging for your call on direction rather than picking one, options:
  (a) Per-model calibrated transform: fit a Breslow-style baseline hazard
      (or similar) from each model's own risk scores on df_train, the way
      lifelines/sksurv do internally for Cox-like models, instead of one
      hardcoded global formula.
  (b) Empirical/KM-based transform: map each model's risk-score decile to
      its own training-set KM-observed event probability (rank-based,
      scale-invariant — sidesteps the magnitude problem entirely, at the
      cost of only being reliable at the decile level, not a smooth
      per-subject probability).
  (c) Leave as-is, but stop reporting Brier/calibration/DCA numbers for the
      6 "approximate" models and clearly mark them out-of-scope for
      publication, keeping only their (unaffected) C-index — narrower
      fix, ships faster, defers the harder calibration-transform problem.
